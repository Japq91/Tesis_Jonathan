Shapiro, M. A., and D. Keyser, 1990: Fronts, Jet Streams, and the Tropopause.
NOAA Technical Memorandum ERL WPL-182, Wave Propagation Laboratory, Boulder, Colorado, August 1990.

EXTRACTO (transcripción literal de la imagen proporcionada por el usuario):

The final low-level flight into the cyclone was made during the period of maximum storm intensity. At this time (~0300 UTC 5 January), the storm central pressure had decreased to ~935 mb and its frontal evolution had progressed to the seclusion phase. The analysis of the 300-m temperature and sea-level pressure, and plotted 300-m wind vectors (space-time adjusted to ~0300 UTC 5 January) are presented as Fig. 25. The 300-m temperature analysis shows that the bent-back warm front and the cold polar air had almost completely encircled the cyclone center, secluding a mesoscale (~100-km) warm pocket of previously post-cold-frontal air near the low center. The seclusion temperatures were ~7-K warmer than those on the cold side of the encircling front. Wind speeds of ~40 m s⁻¹ were observed at the cold side of the frontal seclusion, and the east-west distance across the seclusion, encircling wind speed maximum, and cold air was ~140 km — truly mesoscale.

Cross-section analyses of potential temperature and section-normal wind components were prepared along the lines BB' and AA' of Fig. 25 to describe the vertical structure of the bent-back warm front and frontal seclusion, respectively. The warm-front cross section (Fig. 26a), constructed north of the cyclone center, shows the narrow (~50-km), shallow (~100-mb) bent-back warm front sloping upward and westward, overlying the ~40 m s⁻¹ low-level (~950-mb) wind speed maximum within the marine boundary layer. This frontal structure is similar to that shown in the previous frontal analyses (Fig. 24). Note the shallowness of the strong northerly flow between 57° and 60°W (Fig. 26a) associated with the reversal in wind direction to southerly flow across the frontal zone.

The cross section across the warm-core seclusion and cyclone center (Fig. 26b) illustrates the intensity of the inner core of the mesoscale circulation near the cyclone center and the shallowness of the storm-core structure. Maximum wind speeds north and southerly flows exceeded 35 and 50 m s⁻¹, respectively. The radius of maximum wind speed at 900 mb was 75 km and increased with height to ≥200 km at 700 mb (~3 km). The baroclinicity of the encircling frontal zone sloped outward with height away from the cyclone center.
