# Citas textuales verbatim — mecanismo físico del reparto por cuadrante de viento y precipitación en ciclones extratropicales

Compilación de párrafos originales (sin traducir, sin parafrasear) que sustentan la síntesis de literatura sobre en qué cuadrante/sector respecto al centro del ciclón se concentran los máximos de viento en superficie y/o precipitación, y qué mecanismo físico se invoca. Organizado por mecanismo, igual que la síntesis previa, para que sea fácil ubicar cada bloque temático. La clave de cada entrada (`nombreautor+año`) corresponde al archivo `papers/files_MD/<clave>.md` y a la llave de cita ya usada en la tesis (`\citet{clave}`).

**Advertencia de fidelidad:** varios `.md` de origen provienen de una conversión PDF→texto que pega columnas o palabras sin espacio. Cuando eso ocurrió, las citas se reconstruyeron reinsertando espacios/orden **sin cambiar ninguna palabra**, y se marca explícitamente para que verifiques contra el PDF original antes de usarlas como cita literal en la tesis. Los archivos con este problema son: `catto2010climate`, `gozzo2013air`, `sinclair2023relationship`, `reboita2022from`, `naud2020evaluation`, `laurila2021characteristics` (solo el Abstract).

## 2. Cinta transportadora fría (CCB) → sector frío / trasero / flanco polar

### `catto2016extratropical` (CJ)
> "The CJ occurs on the cold side of the warm front, typically forming just before the minimum cyclone pressure."

### `martinez2014cold`
> "The cold conveyor belt (CCB) is a long-lived synoptic-scale airstream on the poleward (cold) side of the warm front that flows rearward relative to the cyclone motion in the lower troposphere. It extends round the poleward flank of the cyclone and in some mature cyclones it wraps around the west and then equatorward flank where it provides a wind component aligned with the system motion and therefore strong ground-relative winds."

### `schemm2014ccb`
> "The CCB moves close to the surface along the colder side of the bent-back front and experiences a PV increase as it passes below a region of maximum latent heat release at midtropospheric levels. Accordingly, it arrives with high PV values at the tail of the bent-back front where the most intense low-level winds occur. The WCB, which rises above the bent-back front, causes the formation of the midtropospheric heating rate maximum and thereby not only influences the upper-level downstream development, but also drives the increase of PV along the CCB and, in consequence, indirectly drives the formation of the low-level jet at the tail of the bent-back front."

### `priestley2022improved`
> "The CCB (Carlson, 1980; Schultz, 2001) can be identified in cyclone composites of system relative winds at 850-hPa (Figure 2a) traveling rearwards relative to cyclone propagation, polewards of the center... This bias is largest on the westward flank of the cyclone where the CCB tends to fully wrap around the cyclone, forming the low level jet (Schultz, 2001)."
>
> "The overall circulation is cyclonic around the center, however, anticyclonic motion is evident to the southeast of the cyclone center that is associated with the WCB outflow."

### `gramcianinov2023impact` (sector W/NW y S/SW)
> "in the NA, most of the events in mid-latitudes occur in the S, SW sector of the cyclone... this sector of the cyclone is dominated by the cold conveyor belt, located behind the cold front... In the SA (Fig. 7g, h), the analogous situation is observed with the wave extremes occurring mainly in the W and NW sector of the cyclone... Along the Southern Brazilian coast, but in winter, the dominant sector is W/NW, with the mean wave direction and 10-m wind direction from S and SW, in agreement with Gramcianinov et al. (2021a). According to these authors, yet during the winter, there is a prevalence of the cold conveyor belt in early to mature-stage cyclones."
>
> "On average, extreme wave events tend to occur in the cold sector of the extratropical cyclone, to the westward of its centre, where it is located the cold conveyor belt."

### `eisenstein2023identification` (CS/CJ)
> "The CJ is situated south to south-west of the cyclone centre with some statistical overlap with CFC. It occurs closer to the centre than the CS, which dominates the south-west quadrant and can have a distance of up to 1500 km from the cyclone centre."

### `gentile2023observed` (cold sector)
> "some of the strongest and most damaging surface winds form on the rear, equatorward flank of midlatitude cyclones, when the CCB jet wraps around the low-pressure centre and the winds are mixed through the boundary layer producing extreme gusts at the surface, as described in Hewson and Neu (2015)."

### `dacre2023climatology` (CCB)
> "The largest cyclone-relative IVT is found wrapping around the cyclone center, co-located with the bent-back warm front... The CCB is located on the poleward side of the cyclone center. This air is cold and dense and thus the CCB airflow has a low potential temperature and is found in the lower-troposphere."

### `sawada2021heavy` (CCB, cabeza nubosa)

### `gray2020prototype` (CCB envuelve)

## 3. Sting jet → punta de la cabeza nubosa / extremo del bent-back front

### `clark2005sting`
> "This is the second in a series of papers on 'the sting at the end of the tail' — a mesoscale region of potentially damaging surface winds that can occur close to the evaporating tip of a cloud head wrapped around the bent-back front of an extratropical cyclone. [...] The SJ is situated behind the primary cold front, and is distinct from the associated warm-conveyor-belt low-level jet; it is also distinct from the cold-conveyor-belt low-level jet which remains below and behind it as the SJ skirts the bent-back front."
>
> "When the cyclone attains its minimum central pressure, the trailing tip of the cloud head bounding the bent-back front forms a hook which goes on to encircle a seclusion of warm air (Bader et al. 1995). The most damaging winds occur near the tip of this hook — 'the sting at the end of the tail'."

### `clark2018sting`
> "we define the SJ as a coherent air flow that descends from mid-levels inside the cloud head into the frontal-fracture region of a Shapiro–Keyser cyclone over a period of a few hours leading to a distinct region of near-surface stronger winds. It therefore lies above the cold conveyor belt during some stage of its life, but, at least in some cases, descends to reach the top of boundary layer ahead of the cold conveyor belt."
>
> "Behind this, but ahead of the CCB, he also identified a distinct mesoscale region of extremely strong surface winds and, especially, gusts close to the 'tail' of the characteristic 'hook' of the cloud head and distinct from both the WCB and the CCB. [...] He dubbed this mesoscale strong wind region 'the sting at the end of the tail.'"

### `martinez2014cold` (sting jet)
> "The second region of strong winds develops to the southwest and south of the cyclone center as a bent-back front... The air associated with the sting jet descends from the cloud head tip, moving ahead of it around the cyclone into the dry slot behind the cold front."

### `catto2016extratropical` (SJ)
> "The sting jet, first described by Browning [2004], occurs at the end of the bent-back front as high momentum air descends from the cloud head into a region of frontal fracture."

## 4. Trowal / banda "wrap-around" → cuadrante ocluido

### `martin1999forcing`
> "A cyclonically ascending, cloud- and precipitation-producing airstream that originates in the warm-sector boundary layer and flows through the trowal portion of the occluded structure is supported by the upward vertical motions implied by the identified Q-G forcing. This airstream is referred to as the 'trowal airstream' and it is shown to be responsible for the production of the 'wraparound' cloud and precipitation commonly associated with occluded systems."

### `naud2025lifecycle`
> "In a substantial portion of the occluded sector, the quasi-geostrophic (QG) vertical motion ω is attributable to this specific process, which operates exclusively in occluded cyclones. As a result, cloud amount and precipitation are maximized in the TROWAL, not along the surface occluded front."
>
> "The greatest difference relative to non-occluded cyclones is achieved for the 1,000 km radius region, that includes the peak of the warm sector area, with a 42% excess in precipitation for occluded versus non-occluded cyclones."

### `kaylee2025convection`
> "freezing rain occurring within the cyclones' comma head and warm frontal regions. These regions are northwest and northeast of the low pressure center and characterized by complex airflows and mesoscale features."

## 5. Modelo de oclusión Shapiro-Keyser (marco conceptual general)

### `shapiro1999bridge`
> "had strong ascent and precipitation along the bent-back warm front to the west of the cyclone center, and strong moist convection along its cold front."
>
> "was positioned beneath the region of cyclonic speed shear of the left-front (ascending) quadrant of the upper jet exit, downstream from the advancing positive PV anomaly of the upper-level jet."

### `schultz2021antecedents`
> "This model features a right angle between the cold and warm fronts (T-bone), a weakening of the poleward portion of the cold front (frontal fracture), an extension of the warm or occluded front to the rear of and around the cyclone (bent-back front), and the wrapping around of the bent-back front to form a warm-core seclusion of post-cold-frontal air."

**Nota:** este artículo es una revisión histórica de la génesis conceptual del modelo Shapiro–Keyser; no reporta un composite propio de viento/precipitación por cuadrante, por lo que debe citarse solo como marco conceptual, no como evidencia empírica adicional.

### `reboita2022from` (reconstrucción — verificar contra original)
> "warm front (red line in Figure 5f–h), a common feature of the Shapiro–Keyser extratropical cyclone model... Intense warm advection (by the northerly winds over continental South America) towards the warm front also helps to increase frontogenesis in the warm sector, as illustrated in Figure 5e–g."

## 6. Desacople espacial viento-precipitación (evidencia directa de "cuadrantes opuestos")

### `mcerlich2023extremes`
> "The spatial structure shows high precipitation occurrence about the cyclone center that extends in a tail toward the left side of the composite. [...] this tail rotates clockwise throughout the lifecycle. This highlights the warm sector described in idealized models, identifying changes in the position of the warm and cold fronts as the cyclone evolves. Note that because we examine cyclones over the Southern Hemisphere, a clockwise rotation is cyclonic."
>
> "with a more pronounced comma structure in the upper left quadrant of the cyclone. [...] Precipitation occurrence is again greatest during deepening, before rotating clockwise and decreasing during the peak intensity and decay phases."

**Nota:** la cita sobre CAPE en sector cálido que aparecía asociada a este paper en la síntesis previa es en realidad una referencia que McErlich et al. hacen a Pepler & Dowdy (2020), no un hallazgo propio:
> "Pepler and Dowdy (2020) determine that deep moist convective precipitation related to convective available potential energy occurs preferentially in the warm sector of cyclones."

### `pepler2020dimensional` (sur vs. este — desacople estructura/lluvia)
> "heaviest rainfall to the south of the cyclone center for deep surface cyclones but farther east for shallow surface or shallow upper cyclones"

*(Ver también en la Sección 1 las citas de `sinclair2023relationship`, `gentile2025response`, `portal2024linking` y en la Sección 2 la de `naud2020evaluation` — todas documentan que el máximo de precipitación y el de viento no coinciden espacialmente dentro del mismo ciclón.)*

## Papers revisados sin cita verbatim clara o descartados

- `hart2003cyclone` — el uso de "quadrant" es sobre un espacio de fase termodinámico (diagrama Hart), no sobre ubicación geográfica respecto al centro del ciclón.
- `hoskins2005new`, `jones1993climatology` — mencionan cuadrantes/sectores en el contexto de climatología de trayectorias (track density) o de latitudes de una región continental, no de composites individuales de viento/precipitación por ciclón.
- `graf2017objective`, `gramcianinov2019properties` — usan "cuadrante" para geometría de forzantes de PV/omega o climatología de trayectorias, no localización de máximos de viento/precipitación en superficie.
- `corner2025classification`, `coutodesouza2024new`, `coutodesouza2024thesis`, `dalanhese2023new` — menciones de "quadrant"/"sector" no relacionadas con la ubicación de máximos respecto al centro del ciclón (falsos positivos del filtro de palabras clave).
