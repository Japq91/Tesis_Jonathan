ABSTRACT
Composite analysis of the atmospheric mechanisms in South Atlantic explosive cyclones was performed utilising ERA5 reanal-
ysis for the 2010–2020 period. By a manual tracking method, 271 cases were found (127 weak, 104 moderate and 40 intense).
The cyclones follow the preferential displacement from northwest to southeast. There is a trend that, as the explosive cyclone's
intensity increases, its trajectory becomes less zonal and has a more significant impact in the coastal areas of Southern Brazil
and Uruguay. These intensity frequencies were discretized through a compositing technique for studying explosive cyclones. The
results revealed that explosive cyclones begin following the classical model from Bjerknes and Solberg, and, at the end of the ex-
plosive phase, they tend to become Shapiro–Keyser type, especially in moderate and intense ones. Vertical movements appeared
more significantly at the beginning of the explosive stage. It was observed that intense explosive cyclones transition from the
equatorial side of the high- level jet to the polar side. In addition, surface latent heat flux may significantly impact the cyclone's
development both in the beginning and during the explosive phase, especially in intense systems. A horizontal dipole pattern is
seen for surface sensible heat fluxes, as the ocean loses and gains heat in the cold and warm sectors, respectively, at roughly the
same amount.

1   |   Introduction

South America's southern regions have a well- distributed rain-
fall regime throughout the year, favouring agriculture, animal
husbandry,  extractivism,  energy  production  and  freshwater
deposition  on  Earth.  The  passage  of  numerous  weather  sys-
tems,  such  as  extratropical  cyclones,  fronts,  squall  lines,  con-
vective  systems  and  even  tornadoes,  highly  influence  weather
and climate in this area (Reboita et al. 2010; Avila, Nunes, and
Alves 2021).

Among these cited systems, cyclones are the ones that stand
out;  they  are  driven  by  temperature  gradients  between  the
warm  subtropics  and  cold  polar  regions,  which  are  funda-
mental  in  transporting  heat,  humidity,  and  momentum  in
mid- latitudes  (Peixoto  and  Oort  1992;  Field  and  Wood  2007;
Gan  and  Seluchi  2009).  On  the  other  hand,  they  are  often
associated  with  socioeconomic  losses  to  coastal  regions
and  significant  risks  to  offshore  structures  and  sea  routes,
mainly due to wind, wave and precipitation extremes (Allen,
Pezza, and Black 2010; Neu, Akperov, and Bellenbaum 2013;

Recently,  cyclones  have  shown  high  frequency  over  the  conti-
nent  and  the  Brazilian  coast,  causing  severe  damages  such  as
floods,  landslides,  flash  floods,  roof  collapses,  storm  surges
and  even  deaths.  In  addition  to  the  storm  surges  (Seluchi  and
Saulo 1998), depending on the cyclone's position and the wind
intensity,  the  sea  can  recede  from  the  coast,  causing  consider-
able damage.

Because  these  systems  develop  differently  and  have  vary-
ing  attributes  (Wash  et  al.  1992;  Reed,  Grell,  and  Kuo  1993;
Sinclair  and  Revell  2000;  Wang  and  Rogers  2001),  studying
them by analysing  their individuality and extracting  general
characteristics  is  essential  (Rudeva  and  Gulev  2011;  Zhang
et al. 2017; Gramcianinov, Hodges, and Camargo 2019; Ponce
De León and Bettencout 2021). For this, composite fields are
an  attractive  procedure  to  achieve  such  general  characteris-
tics of the studied phenomenon. It involves collecting a certain
number of cases to verify details that would be difficult to de-
tect individually.

Since  these  systems  present  varied  lifetimes  during  develop-
ment,  this  approach  is  encouraging  for  explosive  cyclones  be-
cause  it  standardises  the  explosive  phase  for  a  fixed  period.
Thus, it is possible to obtain a more comprehensive view of how
they behave during the most intense 24 h (Andrade, Nunes, and
Teixeira 2022b).

Therefore, this work aims to identify the main attributes associ-
ated with explosive cyclones in the South Atlantic, according to
their intensity, using composite fields.

Tracking  cyclones  manually  for  11 years  may  be  time-

explosive cyclones. The ERA5 convention is positive downward.
Thus,  negative  surface  heat  fluxes  denote  the  energy  transfer
from the ocean to the atmosphere.

Another  striking  feature  of  latent  heat  fluxes  is  the  appar-

in the literature on whether heat fluxes are important in pre-
conditioning  or  developing  extratropical  systems  (Davis  and
Emanuel 1988; Kuo, Low- Nam, and Reed 1991; Piva, Gan, and
Moscati 2011; Yokoyama and Yamamoto 2019).

The  composite  fields  analysed  indicate  that  explosive  cyclones
present the expected life cycle. They tend to start the develop-
ment according to Bjerknes and Solberg's model, but at the end
of  the  explosive  phase,  they  become  the  Shapiro–Keyser  type.
The baroclinic instability governs these systems during the de-
velopment of the explosive phase, while they become barotropic
at  the  end  of  this  stage.  Ascendant  vertical  movements  occur
more vigorously during the initial time steps, possibly indicat-
ing  other  mechanisms  acting  to  sustain  and  deepen  the  low
pressure.  Moreover,  in  intense  explosive  cyclones,  it  has  been
shown that there is a pattern in the transition from a southeast
core to a north core of the cyclone at the high- level jet, indicat-
ing  that  the  surface  system  passes  from  the  equatorial  side  to
the  polar  side  of  the  upper- level  jetstream.  Surface  heat  fluxes
are essential for developing explosive cyclones in all intensities.
Surface sensible heat fluxes present a clear ‘horizontal dipole’, in
which the ocean loses heat to the atmosphere in the cold sector
at roughly the same amount it gains in the warm sector. Surface
latent heat flux is an important variable contributing to intense
explosive  cyclones, both in the warm and cold  sectors. For in-
tense systems, there is a northwestern contribution for the low
pressure from the initial explosive stage up to 9 h of explosive de-
velopment. The additional moisture supply may deepen the cy-
clone even more and act synergistically with the other variables.
Quantitatively, for weak explosive cyclones, the composite time
series  also  resemble  this  characteristic,  but  not  as  emphasised
as the intense ones. As for the cold sector, the diabatic heating
for all intensities is induced by humidity condensation provided
by the cold advection at the rear of the cyclone in contact with
the warmer ocean. Following the process elucidated by Hirata
et al. (2018), the vapour may be imported into the cyclone, lead-
ing  to  latent  heat  release  and  enhancing  low- level  circulation/
surface pressure.
