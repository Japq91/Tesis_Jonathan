3.1. IdentifyingandTrackingETCs
The ETC tracks are obtained using a cyclone identification and tracking algorithm developed at UQAM
(Chartrand&Pausata,2020;Chen,DiLuca,Winger,Laprise,&Thériault,2022;Chenetal.,2024)andappliedto
theERA5reanalysisdata.TheinputvariablesarehourlyMSLPandrelativevorticityat850hPa(VORS),bothof
whicharespatiallysmoothedusingaCressmanfilterwithaninfluenceradiusof400km.Extratropicalcyclone
centers are identified as the strongest local minima of MSLP within a 200 km radius area, within which the
maximum VORS exceeds 10− 5 s− 1. Extratropical cyclone tracks are produced by matching the previously
identifiedETCcentersthatoccurredinconsecutivehourlytimesteps.Thisisdonebycomputing,foreachcenter,
themostlikelycontinuationbasedonthestorm'spreviousmovementandbyselectingtheclosesttothelastcenter
location.MoredetailsaboutthealgorithmcanbefoundinChen,DiLuca,Winger,Laprise,andThériault(2022).
WeappliedtheETCtrackingtotheregion20°N–80°N,180°W–0°WandonlykeptETCsthatlastedatleast24hr
andtraveledmorethan1,000km,resultinginatotalof11,742ETCtracksduring2001–2020.Chen,DiLuca,
Winger,Laprise,andThériault(2022)showedthattheresultsagreewellwithpreviousstudies,revealingahigh
densityofcyclonesontheleewardsidesoftheRockies,theGreatLakes,theHudsonBay,andalongmaritime
stormtracks,particularlyoffthesoutherncoastofAlaskaandsoutheasternGreenland(seeFigureS3intheSIof
Chen,DiLuca,Winger,Laprise,andThériault(2022)).NotethattheconfidenceofETCdetectionislowerover
highelevationregions,astheETCtrackingalgorithmusesMSLPand850hPavorticity,whichareextrapolated
andmaynotbereliableoverhightopography.Quantitatively,ourcyclonedensityfallswithin,butonthelower
sideof,thereportedrangeof15otherETCalgorithmspresentedinNeuetal.(2013).Wehavemadepublicly
availableourtrackingresultsforanextendedperiodduring1979–2020,alongwithmultipleETCcharacteristics
suchasdifferentintensitymetrics,andtheassociatedPRandWSintheNorthAmericaExtratropicalCyclone
(NAEC)Catalog(Chen,DiLuca,&Winger,2022).

5. Discussion
Ouranalysesdependonsomemethodologicalchoicesthatmeritcloserexaminationduetotheirpotentialimpacts
ontheresults.First,theassociationofextremeswithETCswasmadeusingasingle,fixedradiusof1,000km
aroundtheETCcenterasthisisacommonpracticeintheliterature(e.g.,Chen,DiLuca,Winger,Laprise,&
Thériault,2022;Field&Wood,2007;Leedingetal.,2023;Pintoetal.,2013,2014).Ithasbeenshownhowever
thattheareaaffectedbyETCsvariesstronglythroughouttheirlifecycle(e.g.,Rudeva&Gulev,2007;Simmonds
&Keay,2000),geographically(e.g.,Rudeva&Gulev,2007)andseasonally(Akperov&Mokhov,2010;DiLuca
etal.,2015;Rudeva&Gulev,2007;Simmonds&Keay,2000).Forinstance,wintercyclonesareonaverage
largerthansummercyclones.Therefore,usingafixedradiustorepresentETCs'influencewilllikelyintroduce
someerrors,forexample,underestimatingandoverestimatingETCs'associationwithlocalextremesforwinter
andsummercyclones,respectively.Thiscouldexplainwhyourresultsshowthatthehighestassociationrates
betweenETCsandwindextremesareobservedinsummer(Section4.1.1).Presumably,suchasizedependency
has weaker impacts on the quantification of the ETC‐precipitation association, as extreme precipitation is
characterizedbyfinerspatialscalesthanextremewindsinETCs.
Second,becauseextremeeventscanoccurinclusters,thehighassociationwithETCsmightbeinfluencedbya
few long‐lived weather systems. To assess thepotential impact of clustering, we repeat our analysis after de‐
clustering the identified extreme events, by selecting the more extreme hourly value within a 24 hr window:
foreachgridpoint,startingwiththestrongestevent,subsequenteventsareretainedonlyiftheyoccurredatleast
24hrapartfrompreviouslyselectedstrongerextremes;otherwise,theyareexcluded.Thisde‐clusteringprocess
reducesthenumberof99th‐percentilehourlyextremeeventsbyroughly75%–85%forWSand60%–70%forPR
(not shown).Theassociation between ETCsandextremes decreases slightlyafter de‐clustering,althoughthis
reductionisminorandsmallerthan5%intheNNAregionacrossallseasonsandbothvariables(FiguresS10and
S11 in Supporting Information S1). Furthermore, the de‐clustering has minimal impact on the seasonal and
geographicaldistributionofextremes.Themostnoticeablechangeisarelativeincreaseintheextremeoccurrence
fractioninJJA,butthiswaslimitedto3%inNNAforbothWSandPR.
Third,mostofourresultsarebasedonextremesderivedfromERA5andIMERGdataandafurtherdiscussion
abouttheirstrengthsandweaknessesisrelevant.Regardingwindspeeds,studieshavefoundanunderestimation
tendency at higher magnitudes (Camposet al., 2022;Çalışır et al., 2023). Overeastern North America, Chen
etal. (2024)comparedthelocalextremewindspeedsbetweenERA5andISD,definedasthe95th‐percentile
valueswithin1,000kmradiusofanETC.Whiletheyfoundanaveragednegativebiasof17%forallETCsin
ERA5,ahighcorrelationcoefficientof0.92betweenthesetwodatasetsisalsoidentified,showingthatERA5can
capture the variations of local extremes reasonably well despite the shortage in resolving their magnitude.
Regardingprecipitation,theunderestimationofextrememagnitudesinERA5canbeevenmoresevere(Chen
etal.,2024).However,therepresentationofextremeprecipitationisinherentlymorechallenging.Considerable
discrepanciesarefoundnotonlybetweenreanalysisandobservations,butalsowhencomparingobservational
griddeddatasetsfromdifferentsources(gaugedataandsatelliteretrieval;Timmermansetal.,2019).Basedon
morethan5,000stationsovertheglobe,Laversetal.(2022)assessedERA5'scapabilityintheannualmaximum
1day(RX1)precipitationaccumulationsandfourextremecasesofstormsandflooding,concludingthatERA5
failstoproducethehighestobservedprecipitationtotalsbutcangenerallycapturetheirlocationsandpatterns.In
contrast, IMERG seems to outperform ERA5 in estimating moderate and heavy precipitation (e.g., Jiang
etal.,2023,andourFigure1).Still,IMERGalsocontainsbiases,andseveralstudiesreportedanoverestimation
ofprecipitationintensitycomparedtoin‐situobservationsinmidlatitudes(Moazami&Najafi,2021;Montoya
Duqueetal.,2023;Naudetal.,2018)andadegradedqualityincoldclimates(Tangetal.,2020).Insummary,
while ERA5 has limitations in reflecting the observed extreme magnitudes, partly due to its relatively coarse
spatialresolution(notethatthegriddedvaluesrepresenttheaverageofgridcellratherthanpointmeasurements),
its representation of seasonal extreme occurrence and ETC association generally aligns well with other data
sources.
