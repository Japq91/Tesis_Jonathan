1. INTRODUCTION
Strong surface winds often accompany the low-level jets (LLJs) that occur along
the cold fronts of extratropical cyclones, but there is evidence that the most damaging
surfacewindsoccurinadistinctlydifferentpartofacertainclassofcyclone.Themost
damagingextratropicalcyclonesgothroughanevolutionthatinvolvestheformationofa
bent-backfrontandcloudheadseparatedfromthemainpolar-frontcloudbandbyadry
slot, as in the frontal fracture model of Shapiro and Keyser (1990). When the cyclone
attains its minimum central pressure, the trailing tip of the cloud head bounding the
bent-backfrontformsahookwhichgoesontoencircleaseclusionofwarmair(Bader
etal.1995).Themostdamagingwindsoccurnearthetipofthishook—‘thestingatthe
endofthetail’.
This is the second in a series of papers relating to the sting phenomenon. In the
first paper (Browning 2004: hereafter B2004) the nature of the sting phenomenon—
indeeditsexistenceasadistinctphenomenon—wasestablishedonthebasisofapurely
observational study. The sting terminology was adopted by B2004 following Grøna˚s’
(1995) description of the phenomenon as ‘the poisonous tail of the bent-back front’.
AccordingtoB2004,thecloudheadhasabandedstructureconsistentwiththeexistence
of multiple mesoscale slantwise circulations. Air within these circulations leaves the
hookedtipofthecloudhead(andentersthedryslot)muchfasterthantherateoftravel
of the cloud-head tip, implying rapid evaporation and diabatic cooling immediately

2. THEMODEL AND ITSVALIDATION
ThecasehasbeensimulatedusingtheMetOfficeUnifiedModel(UM)version5.2,
plus modifications equivalent to version 5.3. This is essentially the model run oper-
ationally in the Met Office during autumn 2002. This model solves non-hydrostatic,
deep-atmosphere dynamics using a semi-implicit, semi-Lagrangian numerical scheme
(Cullen et al. 1997). The model includes a comprehensive set of parametrizations,
including boundarylayer(Lock etal. 2000),mixed-phasecloud microphysics(Wilson
andBallard1999)andconvection(GregoryandRowntree1990),withadditionaldown-
draught and momentum transport parametrizations. The model runs on a rotated lati-
tude/longitudehorizontalgridwithArakawaCstaggering,andusesaterrain-following
hybrid-height vertical coordinate with Charney–Philips staggering. A ‘mesoscale’
domainwithhorizontalresolution0.11degrees(approximately12.5km)wasusedwith
250×250 points in the horizontal. This was one-way nested inside a global version
with approximately 60 km resolution at this latitude; the global version ran with the
same 38 levels as used operationally. Runs were performed in the mesoscale version
also at 38 levels, but since we hypothesized that slantwise motions possibly associ-
ated with conditional symmetric instability (CSI, Bennetts and Hoskins 1979) may be
importantinthiscase,runswerealsoundertakenwithenhancedverticalresolutionof90
levels,asusedby LeanandClark (2003).This is consistentwith therecommendations
on resolution made by Persson and Warner (1993). If we assume that structures with
slopes of roughly 1 in 50 are important, then a 12 km horizontal resolution requires
a vertical resolution of about 240 m, which is achieved by the 90-level model in the
mid-troposphere.
